> Achieved 86.7% accuracy on CIFAR-100 using self-supervised contrastive learning without labels.

## Problem

Modern deep learning models heavily rely on large labeled datasets, which are expensive and time-consuming to create. This creates a bottleneck for scaling models to real-world scenarios.

The challenge was simple and high impact: can we learn strong visual representations without labels and still remain competitive on downstream tasks?

## Why this matters

- Labeling large datasets is expensive and slow.
- Real-world domains like medical imaging and autonomous systems often have limited labels.
- Self-supervised learning enables useful learning from large unlabeled corpora.

This project evaluates whether SimCLR can learn meaningful embeddings, improve downstream classification, and reduce dependency on supervision.

## Approach

I implemented the SimCLR contrastive learning framework with the following pipeline.

### Core idea

The encoder learns by pulling positive pairs (two augmentations of the same image) closer while pushing negative pairs (different images) apart.

### Pipeline

1. Data augmentation

  - Random resized crop
  - Color jitter
  - Gaussian blur
  - Horizontal flip

2. Encoder network

  - ResNet-based visual backbone

3. Projection head

  - MLP mapping encoder features into contrastive latent space

4. Loss function

  - NT-Xent (Normalized Temperature-scaled Cross Entropy)

### Training strategy

- Dataset: CIFAR-100
- Pretraining: fully self-supervised
- Evaluation protocol: linear probe (freeze encoder, train linear classifier)
- Training horizon: 200 epochs

## Results

- 86.7% accuracy on CIFAR-100 under linear evaluation
- Strong class-level representation separation in embedding space
- Clear improvement over baseline models trained without contrastive pretraining

## Challenges

### 1. Representation collapse

The model initially drifted toward trivial features. I mitigated this through stronger augmentations and stable normalization settings.

### 2. Batch size sensitivity

SimCLR benefits from large batches. Under constrained GPU memory, I had to tune the training setup carefully for stable optimization.

### 3. Augmentation tuning

Performance was sensitive to augmentation strength. Weak transforms reduced contrastive signal; overly strong transforms removed semantics.

## Key insights

- Contrastive learning can approach supervised performance with the right setup.
- Augmentation policy is central to representation quality.
- Projection heads help training-time latent shaping but are unnecessary during inference.

## What I would improve

- Scale to larger effective batch sizes
- Benchmark SimCLR v2 against BYOL and MoCo under equal compute
- Extend training to higher resolution images
- Introduce multi-GPU distributed training for faster experimentation

## Future work

- Validate on domain datasets such as medical and robotics vision
- Integrate with semi-supervised transfer pipelines
- Deploy embeddings into downstream retrieval and anomaly-detection workflows
