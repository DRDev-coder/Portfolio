## Problem

Modern deep learning workflows often depend on expensive labeled datasets. This limits scalability, especially in domains where annotation is slow or specialized.

## Why it matters

Self-supervised learning lets us exploit large unlabeled corpora and reduce labeling cost while still learning useful representations.

## Approach

I built a SimCLR pipeline with these components:

- Multi-view augmentations: random crop, color jitter, gaussian blur, and horizontal flip
- Encoder backbone: ResNet-based feature extractor
- Projection head: MLP projection into contrastive space
- Objective: NT-Xent contrastive loss

Training ran for 200 epochs, followed by linear evaluation with a frozen encoder.

## Challenges

- Representation collapse risk under weak contrastive signal
- Strong dependence on effective batch size
- Augmentation tuning trade-off between invariance and information loss

## Results

- 86.7% CIFAR-100 accuracy under linear evaluation
- Improved feature separability and downstream generalization
- Better performance than baseline runs without contrastive pretraining

## Learnings

- Contrastive learning is highly sensitive to augmentation design
- Projection heads help representation formation during training
- Stable optimization and evaluation protocol design matter as much as architecture
